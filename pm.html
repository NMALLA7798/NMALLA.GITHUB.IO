Product case study

Exception Copilot: how much should an AI feature do on its own?

A requirements case for an AI triage assistant in a fulfillment network. The question is not whether the model works, but which actions it is safe to take without a person.

Fictional network. Synthetic data: 24,000 exception tickets, 13 weeks, 5 sites. Cost and time assumptions are mine and shown on the page.

Recommendation

Ship in two steps. First, an assist mode that shows the suggested resolution on every non-hazmat ticket. Then gated auto-resolve at confidence 0.95, which holds precision at 97.1% and takes 9% of tickets.

  • Assist mode saves about 106 agent-hours a week (20% of the 525 hours spent on exceptions).
  • Gated auto-resolve adds about 30 hours and $875 a week after rework and escalation costs.
  • The value-maximizing threshold (0.87) auto-resolves 31% of tickets but at only 92.5% precision, which fails the quality floor. The right threshold is a product decision, not a modeling one.
  • Hazmat and compliance tickets never auto-resolve: the model is right on only 50% of them.

Problem and users

Fulfillment exceptions (quantity mismatches, label errors, damage, location conflicts, vendor paperwork, hazmat) are triaged by hand. The network handles about 1,846 a week and agents spend about 525 hours a week on them, 17 minutes per ticket on average.

Exception agents

Need the likely resolution and the evidence in one view, and a fast way to reject a bad suggestion.

Site operations managers

Need backlog, time-to-resolution and the root causes by site and vendor, not individual tickets.

Rollout plan

PHASE 0 · 3 weeks

Shadow mode

Model runs silently on all tickets at two sites. Nothing is shown to agents.

Gate: accuracy within 3 points of offline results.
PHASE 1 · 6 weeks

Assist mode

Suggested resolution and evidence shown on non-hazmat tickets. Agents accept or reject.

Gate: acceptance 60%+, no rise in reopened tickets.
PHASE 2 · 8 weeks

Gated auto-resolve

Label, quantity and location tickets above 0.95 resolve automatically with a 1-minute spot check.

Gate: precision 97%+ by site for 4 straight weeks.
PHASE 3

Network rollout

Expand sites and review thresholds by category every quarter.

Gate: reopen rate under 3%.

Experiment plan

Randomize by site-week, because agents at one site share a queue and would contaminate a ticket-level split. Primary metric: mean time to resolution. Guardrails: precision, reopened tickets, agent satisfaction. Ticket handling time has a standard deviation of 10 minutes, so detecting a 1-minute reduction at 80% power needs about 1,548 tickets per arm (4.2 site-weeks); a 2-minute reduction needs 387. Check the split before reading results.
